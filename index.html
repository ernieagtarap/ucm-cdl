Welcome

Welcome to UCM’s Catalytic Discipleship Lessons.

“Catalytic” means causing a process or event to happen, or increasing the rate at which it happens.

“Catalytic” means causing a process or event to happen or increasing the rate at which it happens. Conducted one-to-one, UCM’s Catalytic Discipleship Lessons (CDL) is a seven-lesson set of foundational Christian assurances and practices designed to help activate and accelerate new believers in their spiritual journey.

CDL also aims to equip believers in disciple-making based on Jesus’ commission to go and do it. Christians are often content being discipled by osmosis, a gradual, usually unconscious process of absorption. We participate in regular Christian activities like attending church, Bible studies, and seminars, trusting that we will grow passively as disciples. The gradual acquisition of knowledge and experience along the Christian journey is right and good. But there is more to grow and mature into. God calls us to the intentional and deliberate act of making disciples.

This CDL resource is meant to help catalyze UCMers into a renewed approach to the work of the church by increasing their confidence in engaging in disciple-making. How does it work? Outlined by UCM’s five core values, each lesson involves a maturing believer and a new believer searching the Scriptures, reflecting on the questions and answers of the faith, and praying. Through the interaction, both individuals may grow deeper in God’s Word, prayer, and disciple-making through modeling and practicing in each lesson.

While disciple-making is refreshing, transformative, and sometimes exhilarating, it is rarely convenient and never perfect. The CDL resource was created as a simple tool to help UCM disciple-making become not only accessible but also a lifestyle, enjoying God while fulfilling his great commission.

Coach’s Guide

Ten pointers for a genuine experience.

Congratulations on taking up the opportunity and challenge of making disciples using CDL as your guide. This engagement with another person can produce good fruit that lasts forever.

  1. Let the Scriptures always be your authority in answering the questions.

    Some learners will need a brief orientation on finding the books, chapters, and verses of the Bible. As appropriate, provide a Bible, a Bible app, or both. For apps, set the version to the ESV (English Standard Version), which UCM has chosen for its discipleship. Point out the NLT (New Living Translation), which learners may like to use to cross-reference.

    The biblical texts are included for quick reference (tap any reference on this site). Have learners answer each question based on what the corresponding text actually states, and complete each lesson in advance of the meeting. Keep with the plain meaning and consensus interpretation of the texts and resist going too deep too fast. If a question arises that you cannot answer, say you are unsure and will get back to them. Point out the glossary.

  2. Usually go through one CDL a week.

    Encourage the learner to undertake the exercises during and after each lesson. Keep sessions to an hour and a half at most. The idea is to finish each lesson wanting more!

  3. Avoid preaching!

    Use questions to discover what learners understand and to stimulate active participation. In disciple-making, you are also growing in your faith; it happens both ways.

  4. Prepare well for each session.

    Be familiar with each lesson’s key concepts and content. Prayer is essential for preparing hearts for the interaction.

  5. Draw out practical implications.

    Ask questions like, “How is this relevant to your life right now?”

  6. Disciple-making is much more than interacting through CDL.

    It implies a life change. This manual is only an initial help. Learners need continual steering in changing their character, ways of thinking, and habits. Pray and ask God to guide you in guiding them.

  7. Help learners take up the habit of prayer.

    Pray together at the beginning and end of each lesson. It is okay if they do not feel comfortable praying out loud at first; trust they will in due time.

  8. Help learners take up the habit of reading the Bible.

    Review the prior Scripture memory exercise at the start of each lesson and ask how the Bible reading is going. Be encouraging.

    Try“How did your exercises go this past week? What went well? What was difficult?”
    Avoid“Did you do your homework?” and the like.
  9. Be sensitive to what God is doing in the learner’s life.

    Take time in each session to answer questions or help with challenges in their personal life. When time runs short, choose the most essential or timely questions to discuss. Skipped questions can be given to learners to search before the next session.

  10. Contact UCM for additional Learner’s Manuals.

    Write to discipleship@unionchurch.ph

Introduction

The Gospel: A Presentation

It is essential for new believers to begin by reviewing the biblical way of having a real relationship with God through Jesus. When a person receives God’s good news of salvation, God sees it not as a mere transaction. Through repentance of sin, believing in God, and receiving his offer of salvation through Jesus, God restores our relationship with him and indwells us by the Holy Spirit, who becomes our teacher, comforter, counselor, and helper, calling us into the new life in Christ (John 3:3).

The Bridge Illustration is a classic gospel-centering and sharing tool. It helps us understand the central message of the faith in its simplicity and clarity across generations and cultures. For new believers and others, such simplicity can be the ultimate sophistication.

In intentional disciple-making, such a tool is essential because Christian people are the only Bible or church some people will ever directly encounter (Matthew 28:18; Romans 10:9-10).

Instructions. Internalize the following presentation by reading and rereading it from the perspective of sharing it with another person, either to present it to someone who wants to know or to review it with a new believer embarking on CDL. Follow the bridge as it builds, then respond to the reflection questions at the end.

  1. We matter to God

    He made us, and he wants to have a relationship with us. However, we have rebelled against God and disobeyed him.

    Romans 3:23 says, “For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.” Our sin has separated us from God and broken off the relationship.

  2. Sin must be penalized

    Furthermore, the sins we have committed must be penalized. The penalty owed is death. Romans 6:23 says, “For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

    Death in the Bible means physical and spiritual death, which means separation from God for eternity in Hell (Matthew 25:41,46).

  3. We can’t earn our way back

    To varying degrees, most of us are aware of our distance from God. So we start doing all kinds of things, like being charitable, attending church, and making sacrifices to get back to God. These are commendable things, but the Bible clarifies that none can earn us God’s forgiveness or re-establish our relationship with him.

    Ephesians 2:8-9 says, “For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast.”

  4. God built a bridge

    It looks pretty hopeless, but the good news is we matter to God. He did for us what we can never do for ourselves. He built a “bridge” by coming to earth as one of us and dying on a cross to pay the death penalty we owed. 1 Peter 3:18: “For Christ also suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, that he might bring us to God…”

    Jesus is the only bridge back to God. In John 14:6, “Jesus said… ‘I am the way, and the truth, and the life, no one comes to the Father except through me.’”

  5. Believe and receive

    This is a picture of the central message of the Bible. God wants us to cross over to the other side. But we need more than to know about this or agree with it. We have to act on it.

    John 3:16: “For God so loved the world…” and John 1:12: “But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God.” Believing and receiving means trusting Jesus Christ alone for your salvation, asking Christ to come and forgive us and lead our lives.

  6. Three promises

    When we transfer our complete trust to Jesus alone, he promises to do three things for us. John 5:24: “Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me (a) has eternal life. He (b) does not come into judgment but (c) has passed from death to life.”

  7. Assurance

    Congratulations! You’re a child of God and, through faith, assured of eternal life. When doubts creep in, remember this promise in John 6:47: “Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever believes has eternal life.”

    Who is speaking here? Jesus. He is God, who died for you so you will have eternal life. He won’t lie to you.

The Assurance of Salvation

Opening question

Can anyone be sure that they are going to Heaven? Briefly explain your answer.

Recite from memory 1 John 5:11-12.

Lesson objective

To give new and spiritually young believers greater confidence in God and the certainty of their eternal life after believing and receiving God’s good news: the assurance of salvation. The Scriptures indicate that God wants Christians to understand that they have certainty of eternal life and oneness with God.

Right at the outset of coming to faith in Christ, God’s adversary, the evil one, will often cast doubt on the renewing work God has done in you. “Submit yourselves… to God. Resist the devil…” (James 4:7). Your internal thoughts may run like this: You are not really saved from sin and forgiven by God just by believing and receiving Christ. That is not enough, and you are not enough.

What will your belief be? Your hope of resisting such spiritual opposition is to resort to God’s Word. God’s mission is to serve the spiritual needs of his creation on earth, to seek, save, and restore what was lost, ultimately to glorify himself.

Objective Assurance of Salvation“Objective”: not influenced by personal feelings, interpretations, or prejudices; existing independent of an observer or thought.

  1. 1. Assurance comes from God through his Word.

    Read aloud 1 John 5:11-12. Based on “the testimony,” God’s written Word, you can be convinced you have Jesus Christ, the Word of God, and with him eternal life. Spiritual opposition may recur, but now you can meet it with God’s Word.

    According to John’s testimony in 1 John 5:11-13,

    Who gives eternal life? God
    Where is eternal life found? in the Son (Jesus Christ)
    Who has eternal life? whoever has the Son
    Who does not have this life? whoever does not have the Son
    What’s the meaning of “having the Son”? believing in the name of the Son of God
    Can you now put this assurance in your own words? God says that I could be sure of eternal life because I have accepted his Son Jesus Christ in my life by believing in him.

  2. 2. Assurance comes from what God has done and is doing for us, that which we cannot do.

    Read Titus 3:4-7. What is the basis of our salvation? goodness and loving kindness of God; his mercy; the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit

    Would we be sure of our salvation if it is based on what we could do? Explain. No. If my assurance of salvation is based on the righteous things I do, I will never be sure since I do unrighteous acts as well. It is like making an omelet from a combination of good and rotten eggs; it will never be acceptable.

  3. 3. Assurance comes from faith in God’s promises.

    Assurance springs not from the power of positive thinking but from the power of God’s good news, the gospel. Assurance of salvation is primarily grounded in God’s promises. In Romans 4:21, faith takes God at his word.

    State Romans 8:38-39 in your words: Nothing and no one, no situation or experience in the past, present, and future can separate me from God’s love in Christ Jesus.

    Trust is the basis of any close relationship. Our trust will be strengthened by getting to know who God truly is and seeking to commune with him daily through prayer and his Word.

    Some people may not feel fully forgiven, or feel unworthy or unlovable, perhaps believing their sins were too great, feeling ashamed, or discovering they cannot lead a sinless life even with the Holy Spirit. Do not be discouraged! The gospel is not about “sin management” but receiving and pursuing a real relationship with God in Jesus Christ. We have been given the Spirit to strengthen us when we are weak (2 Corinthians 12:9-10).

    Victorious Christian living is covered in Lesson 3; the trustworthiness of God’s Word in Lesson 5.

Subjective Assurance of Salvation“Subjective”: belonging to the thinking subject rather than to the object of thought; the result of personal feelings, tastes, or opinions.

  1. 1. Assurance also comes from the Holy Spirit, who testifies directly to the believer’s spirit that we are children of God.

    Read Romans 8:15-16. What does the Spirit enable the believer to do? The Holy Spirit enables the believer to accept that s/he has been adopted by God.

    What does the Spirit do with our spirit? The Holy Spirit assures us that we are children of God.

    Read John 7:38-39. How can you know you have the Holy Spirit? if we believe in Christ Jesus

    More on the nature and work of the Spirit is covered in Lesson 4.

  2. 2. Assurance comes from the evidence of a transformed life.

    The transformed life can function as a sign and subjective form of assurance.

    Read 1 John 2:3. What testifies that one knows one belongs to God? when one keeps Christ’s commandments

    Read 1 John 3:14. What evidence could one show that s/he has passed from death to life? S/he loves the children of God (brothers and sisters in Christ).

    Read 2 Peter 1:3-11. How does a believer confirm or make sure of their salvation? S/he experiences development of Godly qualities in their life.

Dealing with doubt in your new relationship with Christ

Even though feelings are important, your relationship with Christ is based on facts, not on feelings that can change daily. Doubt, questions, and varying emotions are common parts of everyone’s life, but they are unreliable gauges of anyone’s relationship with Christ. The Christian lives by faith in the trustworthiness of God and his Word.

The Assurance of Forgiveness

Opening question

Describe a time when you “missed the mark,” failed, or fell short on something you wanted to do.

Recite 1 John 1:9.

Lesson objective

This lesson helps increase our trust in God and the certainty of forgiveness in receiving Jesus Christ as Savior and Lord.

Having a real relationship with Jesus means we open our lives to be completely transparent and honest before him. When we falter, we can admit our shortcomings to God, and his forgiveness is guaranteed to us. When we begin a relationship with Jesus, we are given a new heart to want to live pleasingly to God (Ezekiel 36:26). But while we still live in this fallen world, there will be times when we fall short and give way to sin, and the evil one may accuse us in our thoughts: Aren’t you supposed to be a Christian? Christians don’t do those things. Just give up on this whole thing.

Sin & its effect

  1. 1. What is sin in your own words?

    Anything that falls short of conformity to God’s standard and will

  2. 2. Read Psalm 51:1,2,12. What happens when we sin? What do we lose?

    We lose the joy of our salvation. We do not lose our salvation but cannot enjoy it when sinning.

    Read Ephesians 4:29-31. What happens with the Spirit of God when we sin? We grieve the Holy Spirit of God.

    Read 1 John 1:8-9. What should we do when we fall short and sin? We should confess our sin.

    To confess a sin means acknowledging, uncovering, and calling it exactly what God calls it. When we honestly confess a sin before God, we are willing to forsake the sin by turning away from it.

    What two things does God promise to do when we confess our sins to him? He forgives us our sins and cleanses us from all unrighteousness.

    What is your reflection on this section?

Biblical & true forgiveness

  1. 3. What does biblical, true forgiveness mean?

    1. A

      Holding no fault or offense against a person. What does Psalm 103:10 say about God? God does not deal with us according to our sins nor repay us according to our iniquities; he does not give us the punishment we deserve.

    2. B

      Having no animosity toward a person (ill will, hatred, resentment, hostility). Read Romans 5:6-8. What does God do for us? He showed his love to us when we were hopelessly trapped in our sins and disobedience. Christ died for us.

    3. C

      Being able to leave the fault or offense right where it is, in the past, and move on. What does Psalm 103:12 say about God? He removed our transgressions from us.

      How is God’s forgiveness described in Hebrews 8:12? He will no longer remember our sins.

  2. Biblical illustration

    Read Luke 15:11-32: the Parable of the Prodigal Son. We see the loving kindness of the father that moved him to forgiveness. We also see the unforgiving heart of the older brother, which reminds us of our appropriate response to another’s offense: “forgive us our debts, as we also have forgiven our debtors” (Matthew 6:12). What is your response to the story?

A.C.T.S. prayingBased on elements of the prayer Jesus taught his disciples.

Confessing our sins before God and receiving his forgiveness is something we can do as part of our regular prayer life. Each time we meet with God, we can ask him to search our hearts and reveal any sin we can confess so that there will be no barrier between us (Psalm 139:23-24). Here is a prayer model that incorporates confession into time spent with God:

  • AAdoration. Praise God for who he is!
  • CConfession. Ask God to show you sins you may not even realize, and receive forgiveness.
  • TThanksgiving. Thank God for everything he has done, is doing, and will do in your life.
  • SSupplication. Present your requests to God.

Begin praying. As you do, know that God, our loving Father, is delighting in you! What is your reflection on this section?

The Assurance of Victorious Christian Living

Opening question

Describe a time you won something. It can be anything. What did achieving victory feel like?

Share your faith story briefly and recite 1 Corinthians 10:13.

Lesson objective

This lesson helps raise awareness of the privilege of enjoying God’s salvation and forgiveness through our daily decision to walk with God. Spiritual growth happens when we pursue God across the “hills and valleys” of life. The authentic Christian life is faithful, makes spiritual progress, and perseveres.

“And without faith it is impossible to please him, for whoever would draw near to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him.”Hebrews 11:6

The overflowing effects of our faith in Jesus enable us to walk victoriously in our Christian lives.

God’s promise of victory

  1. 1. Read 1 Corinthians 15:57. What does God give us?

    He gives us victory through Jesus Christ.

  2. 2. Read 1 John 5:4-5. What does it mean that he gives us victory versus striving for it?

    Believing that Jesus is the Son of God, fully trusting him (faith), gives us victory

  3. 3. Read 1 Corinthians 10:13.

    What does victory mean in this context? victory over temptation and sin

    What common experience do we have? temptation

    “Temptation” depicts the moment when we feel seized and squeezed by external or internal forces designed to bring us down.

    What does God promise concerning temptation? He will provide the way of escape that we may be able to endure it.

    The Plimsoll line is a reference mark on a ship’s hull that indicates the maximum depth to which the vessel may be safely immersed when loaded with cargo. “God is faithful, and he will not let you be tempted beyond your ability…” (1 Corinthians 10:13).

    God will always provide a way to escape whatever temptation stands before us. If we look for a way to avoid whatever sin compels us, God promises we will find it.

  4. 4. How can we live victoriously?

    Read John 16:33: accept the fact that Christ has already won for us the victory

    Read 1 John 5:5: believe that Jesus is the Son of God

Keys to victorious Christian livingRead Ephesians 4:17-24.

  1. 1. Recognize the kind of world we are living in.

    How did Paul describe the not-yet believers (verses 18–19)? Paul called them Gentiles who live in the futility (pointlessness or uselessness) of their minds.

    Here we find the not-yet believers’ worldview, state of mind, and way of life. Enumerate the characteristics. darkened in understanding; alienated from the life of God; ignorant due to hardness of heart; callous; given up to sensuality; greedy to practice every kind of impurity

    Have you observed any similarities in your own life? Give a specific example.

  2. 2. Acknowledge that we are prone to follow the dictates of this world.

    What is Paul’s exhortation to the believers (verse 17)? They should no longer live as the Gentiles do.

    Though we are no longer of this world, we are still in this world, which means we can still be affected by it in how we think and act. Paul’s exhortation and testimony are illuminating: “…let anyone who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall” (1 Corinthians 10:12); also see Romans 7:18b-19, which invites us to live with caution, but we need not live in fear. We can trust the Holy Spirit to guide us into all truth as he promises (John 16:13).

    In what ways do we allow “the world” and its way of life to sway us to think and live like we are still of the world? Example? by allowing our lives to be saturated by the things and ideals of the world instead of knowing and practicing what the Bible exhorts us to have and do

  3. 3. Subscribe to God’s way and cooperate with him.

    What are we to put off (Ephesians 4:22-23)? our old self, our former manner of life that was dictated by corrupt and deceitful desires

    What are we to put on (Ephesians 4:24)? the new self, characterized by righteousness and holiness

    We “put on” when we received Christ as Savior and Lord. We are given the “position” as children of God, and this never changes. But “putting off” is a repeating experience. It relates to our “state,” our daily observance as followers of Christ, and it happens not by our strength or striving (2 Corinthians 12:9-10). When we accept this weakness, he gives us the favor and strength to make choices in line with his truth: “…for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure” (Philippians 2:13).

How we subscribe to God’s ways of life

  1. 1

    We discard our old habits, identifying and consciously letting go of sinful patterns from our former way of life.

  2. 2

    We clothe ourselves in Christ, embracing the righteousness and holiness of Christ as our new identity.

  3. 3

    We renew our minds by regularly meditating on God’s Word and praying to allow God to transform our thinking into his likeness.

  4. 4

    We give God full access to our hearts. Jesus says, “For out of the heart come evil thoughts, murder, adultery, sexual immorality, theft, false witness, slander” (Matthew 15:19). We need Jesus to touch and transform our hearts. We can give him total access by inviting him in and asking him to show us what needs his healing (John 15:4).

The Person and Work of the Holy Spirit

Opening question

What significant voices have you listened to in your life thus far (parents, pastors, spouse, biblical authors, favorite preachers and teachers, friends, etc.)?

Recite John 14:26.

Lesson objective

This lesson helps us recognize that with the good news, we have received the Holy Spirit, who continuously renews, strengthens, and guides us in our lives of faith. We have the Holy Spirit in our trials and to minister to others.

The person of the Holy Spirit

  1. 1. What does the Spirit possess that shows he is a person?

    Ephesians 4:30he has emotion: we could grieve him
    John 14:26he demonstrates intelligence as he teaches and guides believers
    1 Corinthians 12:11he decides based on his intent, “as he wills”

    The Holy Spirit possesses emotion, intellect, and will, the three fundamental attributes of a person. That means we refer to him as “he” rather than “it.”

  2. 2. What attributes or traits of deity does the Spirit possess?

    Psalm 139:7everything is in his immediate presence; no one, nothing escapes his presence
    Zechariah 4:6power belongs to him
    1 Corinthians 2:10-11, Psalm 139:2-6he is all-knowing

    Based on these passages, who then is the Holy Spirit? The Holy Spirit possesses characteristics that only God possesses; he is therefore God.

    Now that we have learned about the Holy Spirit’s nature, let us turn to his work.

The work of the Holy Spirit

  1. 1. What works does the Spirit do that only God can do?

    Job 33:4he is our Creator, he is the Giver of life
    Titus 3:5he saves us
    2 Thessalonians 2:13-14he sanctifies us

  2. 2. What is the relationship of the Holy Spirit with believers?

    1. A

      He brings conviction to the not-yet believer and causes them to see the gospel’s truth. Read John 16:8-11.

    2. B

      He indwells the believer permanently. Read 1 Corinthians 6:19 and 1 Corinthians 6:20.

    3. C

      He seals the believer. Read 2 Corinthians 1:22, Ephesians 1:13, and Ephesians 4:30.

    4. D

      He sovereignly bestows spiritual gifts or abilities to every believer to build up the church and be the church to a lost and broken world. Read 1 Corinthians 12:4-11.

  3. 3. What else does the Holy Spirit do?

    1. A

      Helps us to know and carry out God’s will (Ezekiel 36:26-27)

    2. B

      Makes God’s presence evident in us (John 7:38-39, John 14:23)

    3. C

      Serves as Help that will remain with us forever (John 14:16)

    4. D

      Reminds us of Jesus’ words (John 14:26)

    5. E

      Offers guidance into the truth and tells us what is ahead (John 16:13)

    6. F

      Gives us power beyond our strength (Luke 24:49)

    7. G

      Enables us to be witnesses carrying out Jesus’ ministry of healing the sick and setting the captives free (Acts 1:8)

    8. H

      Fills us with the life of Christ (Romans 8:11)

    9. I

      Reminds us of our identity as God’s sons and daughters (Romans 8:14-15)

    10. J

      Helps us to pray and make intercession (Romans 8:26)

    11. K

      Helps us live freely, animated, and motivated by God’s Spirit instead of selfishness (Galatians 5:16-17)

    12. L

      Transforms our character into the image of Christ (Galatians 5:22-24; 2 Corinthians 3:18)

Rooted in Scripture

We devote ourselves to understanding, obeying, and delighting in God’s Word.

The Trustworthiness of the Bible

Opening question

“All sources of information about God are of equal authority and significance.” True or false?

Recite from memory 2 Timothy 3:16.

Lesson objective

This lesson helps us further comprehend the gospel by increasing our reliance upon God’s Word as the ultimate authority and standard of faith and practice.

The source of the Bible

What phrase describes Scripture as being from God? breathed out by God

What does this mean to you? The Bible is from God and therefore I should consider it my ultimate authority for my faith and Christian living.

Read 2 Peter 1:20-21. Where do prophecies of Scripture come from? No prophecy was ever produced by the will of man; the Holy Spirit “carried along” those who spoke prophecy.

What phrases are repeated in these similar verses: Deuteronomy 5:27; Isaiah 40:1; Isaiah 57:21; Jeremiah 42:20; Ezekiel 34:31; Psalm 75:2? God says, God speaks, God declares

“All Scripture is breathed out by God” (2 Timothy 3:16) and “…as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit” (2 Peter 1:21) mean that God inspired and superintended the human authors, so they wrote what he intended. Therefore, the Bible we have is true, accurate, and reliable. Are we supposed to take the Bible’s word for it about its own nature and character? Yes. Personal experience and testimonies of believers point to the fact that once a person receives Jesus Christ as Savior and Lord, there is an unmistakable openness and receptivity to the Bible, primarily caused by the indwelling presence of the Holy Spirit, who convicts and teaches us.

The benefits of the Word

    The Bible tells us when we are off the right track, directs us back to the right track, and enables us to stay on it.

1. Hearing the Word from pastors and teachers provides fresh insight into the Scriptures. The weakest finger represents hearing, because we retain only 5 percent of what we hear. Romans 10:17

2. Reading gives us an overview of the Bible and is the foundation of a regular quiet time. We generally retain 15 percent of what we read. Revelation 1:3

3. Studying the Scriptures deepens our conviction. It requires greater time and effort but results in increased Bible knowledge. Most people retain 35 percent of what they study. Acts 17:11

4. Memorizing the Word enables us to use the Sword of the Spirit to overcome temptations and to have verses ready for ministering to others. The index finger, our strongest, represents memorization. We remember 100 percent of what we memorize if we consistently review it. Psalm 119:9-11

5. Meditation is the inward process that accompanies each of the other four. Only the thumb can touch all the other fingers. By meditating on God’s Word as we hear, read, study, and memorize, we discover its transforming power at work in us. Psalm 1:2-3

Interacting with God’s WordThe S.O.A.P.S. Bible reading method

As followers of Jesus, we can commune with God daily through his Word. A fair guideline is to read five or more chapters each week. But how do I apply what I learn? Keeping a journal using the S.O.A.P.S. method can help you understand, observe, and delight in God through his Word.

  • SScripture. Write out one or more verses that are particularly meaningful to you today.
  • OObservation. Rewrite those verses or key points in your own words to better understand.
  • AApplication. Consider what God might be communicating to you and how you might observe and apply it.
  • PPrayer. Write a prayer expressing your honor, love, and thanksgiving to God, and how you plan to incorporate what you received.
  • SSharing. Ask God if there is anyone he wants you to share with about what you learned.

An example

S “For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways,” declares the LORD. “For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways and my thoughts than your thoughts” (Isaiah 55:8-9).

O As a human being, I am limited in what I know and in how to do what I know. God is not limited in any way. He sees and knows everything; nothing is impossible for him.

A Since God knows everything and his ways are best, rather than relying on my way of doing things, the better way is to follow him.

P God, I don’t know how to live a good life that pleases you and helps others. My ways can lead to mistakes, and my thoughts can lead to hurt. Please teach me your ways and your thoughts instead. Holy Spirit, please help me.

S I will share these verses and this application with my friend, Steve, who is going through a difficult time and might need direction for essential decisions he’s facing.

The Scriptures are not meant to serve as a static “letter of the law” like the Pharisees used to observe his commands. The Bible is living and active (Hebrews 4:12), enabling us to encounter the fellowship of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. We are called to understand, observe, and delight in God’s Word, not only for head knowledge but as God’s Word that meets us wholeheartedly in growing our relationship with him and others (Luke 24:32).

The Fellowship of Believers

Opening question

The term “Church” is the body of believers we are joined with the moment we come to faith in Christ. What are the features of this living group of people?

The nature of the Church

  1. 1. The Church is invisible.

    The Church has been the community of all true believers since God first poured out his Spirit on them. It is invisible in its true reality as the fellowship of all believers since that time; we cannot see it as God sees it.

    How is the church described in Hebrews 12:1,23? a great cloud of witnesses; the assembly of the firstborn who are enrolled in heaven… the spirits of the righteous made perfect

  2. 2. The Church is visible and universal (as Christians on earth see it).

    The visible Church is the group of people who come together each Sunday to worship, profess faith in Christ, and live and act as a community of believers. The Bible also encourages us to come together as often as we can. One way to do this at UCM is to join one of the church’s Small Groups: close fellowship times including food together, sharing testimonies, praising God, helping each other out, praying, and inviting others to join in. These groups are one of the main avenues through which we grow and mature in our faith.

    The invisible Church joins the visible, universal Church in worship and is composed of all believers throughout the entire world at a given time (1 Corinthians 12:13; Ephesians 3:14-15).

The Advancement of God’s Kingdom

Opening question

Lesson objective

This last lesson helps raise awareness of the kingdom of God. As God has loved us, we love others by stewarding what God has given us to participate in his kingdom’s advancement by fulfilling our collective and individual disciple-making mission.

“Seek not to understand that you may believe, but believe that you may understand.”Saint Augustine

The meaning of the kingdom of God

The Bible’s basic meaning of “kingdom” is God’s kingly rule: his reign, lordship, and sovereign governance. Basically, it is a way of ordering things. Look up the following verses and fill in the blanks.

  1. 1. Psalm 103:19. It is the rule of God on earth and in heaven.

  2. 2. Luke 4:43. God’s kingly rule involves a saving and a redeeming activity on behalf of his people, which is why the coming of the kingdom in the New Testament is called good news.

  3. 3. Mark 1:15. Repent and believe in the gospel is the key to entrance into God’s kingdom.

First, the present aspect. Jesus taught his disciples to pray, “…your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven” (Matthew 6:10). When we pray and seek God’s kingdom, we also pray for his rule and reign in our lives. Jesus said, “…behold, the kingdom of God is in the midst of you” (Luke 17:21), speaking of himself. When you are under his lordship, and when he is in control of your life, that is God’s kingdom (Romans 8:17; Ephesians 3:6).

Second, the future aspect. We pray for a day when God will return to restore all things (Acts 3:21). The new heaven and new earth will be established (Revelation 21:1), and Christ will reign in his eternal kingdom forever. As believers, we will participate in that with him forever; this is a future vision we hope for and hold onto.
